# Collar Sleeve Lazy Lasso vs Standing Pass

## Overview
A guard drill where the guard player starts with collar and sleeve grips, a lazy lasso hook over the sleeve-controlled arm, and the other foot on the opposite bicep, while the top player starts standing. The guard player works to off-balance the standing passer and attack with omoplatas, triangles, armbars, and kimuras or sweep, while the top player works to clear the lasso and grips and pass to a connected chest-to-chest or chest-to-back position.

## Details
**Time:** 3 minutes, 2 partners or 6 minutes total - switching back and forth

### Guard Player
**Position:** On your back with a collar grip and a sleeve grip, a lazy lasso hook over the sleeve-controlled arm (shin over the arm, foot not fully wrapped underneath), and your other foot on the bicep of your partner's collar-side arm, with your partner standing  
**Objective:** Use the lasso and grips to break your partner's posture and off-balance them, attacking with an omoplata, triangle, armbar, or kimura, or sweeping to the top position  
**Win:** Successfully lock up or finish an omoplata, triangle, armbar, or kimura (depending on comfortability), or sweep and come on top

### Top Player
**Position:** Standing inside your partner's guard with your collar and sleeve controlled, one arm caught in a lazy lasso hook, and a foot on your other bicep  
**Objective:** Strip the grips, clear the lasso hook and the foot on your bicep, and pass the guard  
**Win:** Successfully pass the guard and establish chest-to-chest or chest-to-back contact with your hands connected

## Instructions
1. Guard player starts on their back and establishes the collar grip, sleeve grip, lazy lasso hook over the sleeve-controlled arm, and the other foot on the opposite bicep
2. Top player stands up inside the guard with the grips and hooks already in place
3. Guard player works to off-balance the standing partner and create angles for omoplata, triangle, armbar, or kimura attacks
4. Top player works to strip the grips, unwind the lasso, and clear the legs while staying based and out of submissions
5. If the guard player loses the lasso or a grip, they work to recover it or transition to another attack
6. The round ends when either player achieves their win condition
7. Reset and repeat, switching roles after the predetermined time

## Coaching Points
For the guard player:
- The lazy lasso is there to control the shoulder and rotate your partner - it does not need to be a deep wrap to do its job
- Use the collar grip to keep your partner from standing tall and stepping back out of your legs
- Your foot on the bicep keeps that arm away from your legs and stops them from grabbing a pant or clearing the lasso hook
- When your partner postures up, follow their posture with your hips and swing to the omoplata angle rather than staying flat
- The lasso side leg threading through is your omoplata; the free leg coming over the shoulder is your triangle - read which arm they give you
- Scoot your hips under your partner when they base back, and elevate them when they step forward
- Stay mobile - against a standing passer, hip movement matters more than grip strength

For the top player:
- Keep your knees bent and your weight centered; standing tall with straight legs lets the guard player break you down
- Address the lasso hook early - the longer it stays, the more your shoulder gets rotated
- Strip one grip at a time and control the hand afterward so it does not immediately re-grip
- Circle toward the lasso side to unwind the hook rather than pulling straight back against it
- Keep the elbow of the lasso-controlled arm pinned to your side to limit the omoplata and kimura
- Once you clear the legs, close the distance immediately and connect your hands before the guard player recovers
